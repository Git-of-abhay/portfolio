Building software for a real operation means the interface, APIs, data model, and day-to-day work all have to fit together. During my Software Development Engineer internship at Ecowell India, I helped build and launch a customized CRM for more than 10,000 customers across 8 locations.

## The scale was organizational as well as technical

The system coordinated workflows across 18 teams and 8 role-based access levels. It supported sales, service, attendance, payments, and logistics. That meant the implementation had to reflect who could see an item, who could act on it, and what should happen next.

I worked across web and mobile applications using JavaScript, TypeScript, React, and React Native, backed by Node.js, Express.js, PostgreSQL, and SQL. The platform integrated 48 API endpoints and supported 1,000+ daily tickets and more than 10,000 transactions.

## Details that affect people in the field

GPS-based attendance and real-time workforce tracking served more than 1,000 employees. Mapping APIs, role-aware visibility, and location-driven business rules were part of making those features useful in daily operations.

The work also included automating over 70 multi-stage workflows. Within a three-person engineering team, I coordinated frontend, backend, database, and cross-platform dependencies while helping take the product to launch.

## What I carry into the next build

I like engineering work that connects a clear user need with dependable implementation. A system is valuable when people can rely on it to complete the work in front of them—at the scale they actually operate.
